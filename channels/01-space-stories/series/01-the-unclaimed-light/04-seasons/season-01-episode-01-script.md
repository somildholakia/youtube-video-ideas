## Director's intent

Open on scale and unease, not exposition. The audience should feel that the universe is beautiful but indifferent. The crew are skilled adults with different disciplines, not quipping archetypes. Keep the signal physically observable: changes in phase, instrument behaviour and route geometry. Do not show a face, creature or humanoid entity behind it. The final reveal should be quiet and precise: the signal has altered its pattern to include a coordinate that was not present before the crew's decision.

**SH01E01-SH003, 00:00:08.000–00:00:12.000.** Close view of a thin ring of dust around the star. One section of dust moves against the rest.

## SEQUENCE 2 — WAYFARER IN TRANSIT (03:30–06:30)

**VISUAL.** Exterior wide: SHIP001 Wayfarer moves through a sparse Lumen Trail, a faint distortion around the ship rather than a glowing tunnel. The ship is small against the geometry.

**AUDIO AU005 — MUSIC.** Introduce a restrained original motif: three widely spaced notes, warm but unresolved. No resemblance to an existing franchise theme.

**INT. WAYFARER — OBSERVATION DECK.**

MARA stands at the forward glass. She is not posing; she is checking a physical reflection against a sensor readout.

**MARA (AU006, calm, low):** “How long until the corridor closes?”

**ORI (AU007, even, precise):** “At current drift, eleven minutes. Confidence interval: plus or minus forty seconds.”

**MARA:** “Give me the interval again when it becomes nine.”

Across the room, ILYAN studies a phase plot. NIA is seated at the navigation console, one hand resting on a manual control. TOMAS speaks through an open maintenance channel. SERA watches a sample container secured inside a vibration-damped case.

**ILYAN:** “The corridor is repeating a phase offset.”

**NIA:** “Repeating how?”

**ILYAN:** “Exactly.”

**NIA:** “That is not an answer.”

**ILYAN:** “It is the part I trust.”

Tomas enters, carrying a tool case.

**TOMAS:** “If the ship starts doing anything exactly, tell me before it does it to the engines.”

A small laugh from Sera. Mara does not smile, but her shoulders loosen.

**SERA:** “You all talk about the corridor like it is a machine.”

**ILYAN:** “It behaves like one.”

**SERA:** “So does a storm, until you stand in it.”

## SEQUENCE 3 — THE CHARTER (06:30–10:00)

**INT. BRIEFING SPACE.**

The crew reviews a survey charter. The assignment is modest: verify a faint gravitational anomaly at the edge of a charted route. The system has no registered settlements and no confirmed biosignatures. Do not call it “empty” as a fact; the official file says “no confirmed activity.”

**MARA:** “We verify the reading, mark the corridor, and come home. No landing unless we have a reason. No samples without a contamination plan.”

**SERA:** “And no declaring a place lifeless because our instruments are bored.”

**MARA:** “Agreed.”

Ilyan enlarges a phase trace.

**ILYAN:** “This is not the anomaly in the charter.”

**TOMAS:** “Then why are we looking at it?”

**ILYAN:** “Because the charter's anomaly is sitting on top of this.”

He overlays two measurements. The traces align, then diverge by a fraction.

**NIA:** “That is a route marker.”

**ILYAN:** “It resembles one.”

**NIA:** “You physicists always say that when the pilot is right.”

**ILYAN:** “And pilots always say that before the maths catches up.”

Mara notices the divergence is changing in real time.

## SEQUENCE 4 — THE IMPOSSIBLE READING (10:00–13:30)

**INT. SENSOR BAY.**

Ilyan and Tomas isolate the reading from the ship's primary navigation stack. They run a second sensor using a different power source. The result repeats.

**TOMAS:** “Independent circuit. Same result.”

**ILYAN:** “Then it is not a software echo.”

**ORI:** “Correction: the data is not generated by ship software. Origin remains unclassified.”

Sera examines a sample container. Its liquid surface has a subtle oscillation matching the phase trace.

**SERA:** “This container is isolated from the sensor deck.”

**ILYAN:** “It should not be responding.”

**SERA:** “I did not say it was responding. I said it is moving.”

The liquid settles. No one touches it.

**MARA (comms):** “Report.”

**ILYAN:** “The anomaly is structured. It is embedded in the corridor's phase behaviour.”

**MARA:** “Can we map it without entering?”

**ILYAN:** “Not before the window closes.”

A pause.

## SEQUENCE 5 — A ROUTE THAT SHOULD NOT EXIST (13:30–17:00)

**INT. NAVIGATION.**

Nia displays two possible exit paths. One is safe but returns the ship to the original survey corridor. The second is not on the chart and appears only when the signal is included in the model.

**NIA:** “There is a branch.”

**MARA:** “There is a modelled branch.”

**NIA:** “It has a stable envelope.”

**ILYAN:** “For four minutes.”

**TOMAS:** “That is not stable. That is a deadline.”

ORI reports a growing shear value. The ship's external sensor assembly begins to vibrate.

**ORI:** “The uncharted branch is converging with our current vector.”

**MARA:** “Can we hold position?”

**NIA:** “Not without losing both windows.”

The crew is silent. Mara asks for independent risk estimates. Each gives a different answer. Sera points out that the signal could be a natural process and that entering it may disturb the system. Ilyan argues that a single remote measurement cannot establish that.

## SEQUENCE 6 — THE CHOICE TO ENTER (17:00–21:00)

Mara orders a short passive scan. The signal changes while the scan runs. It now contains a second phase interval that matches the uncharted branch.

**ILYAN:** “It changed.”

**MARA:** “The signal?”

**ILYAN:** “The route.”

**NIA:** “No. The route was there. Our model did not have the right variable.”

Mara studies the data. The decision is hers; she refuses to pretend the risk is solved.

**MARA:** “We take the branch. Minimum thrust. No active transmission. If the envelope narrows, Nia aborts without waiting for me.”

**NIA:** “Understood.”

**MARA:** “I mean it.”

**NIA:** “So do I.”

## SEQUENCE 7 — CROSSING THE TRAIL (21:00–25:00)

**EXT. WAYFARER / LUMEN TRAIL.**

The trail does not look like a tunnel. Stars bend by a few degrees; the ship's silhouette briefly doubles at the edges. Keep the image grounded and readable.

**AUDIO AU008 — MUSIC.** The three-note motif returns, but the second note is delayed. Add low mechanical strain and a short pressure flutter.

**INT. ENGINEERING.**

Tomas monitors power. One sensor bank drops offline. ORI reroutes power, but the reroute creates a heat spike.

**TOMAS:** “I need the forward array quiet.”

**ORI:** “The forward array is required for navigation.”

**TOMAS:** “And it is currently cooking its own mount.”

**MARA (comms):** “Do it.”

**TOMAS:** “You heard the captain. Go dark.”

The ship shudders. Nia corrects with manual controls.

**NIA:** “Trail is moving under us.”

**ILYAN:** “It should not move.”

**NIA:** “Tell it that.”

A hard but controlled transition. The stars return to ordinary positions.

## SEQUENCE 8 — KESTREL NULL (25:00–29:00)

The ship exits into a system with one dim star, two small planets and a broad field of fine debris. No obvious structures, no artificial lights, no confirmed biosignatures.

**ORI:** “System classification unavailable. No matching catalogue entry.”

**MARA:** “Search adjacent designations.”

**ORI:** “No adjacent designation.”

**SERA:** “What does the environment say?”

**ORI:** “No confirmed biological signature.”

**SERA:** “That is not what I asked.”

She looks out at the planet. A thin atmospheric line catches the star.

**SERA:** “It is beautiful.”

**TOMAS:** “That is not a sensor reading.”

**SERA:** “No. It is why we came.”

## SEQUENCE 9 — THE SIGNAL CHANGES (29:00–33:00)

Ilyan compares the pre-entry and post-entry signal. The pattern is different. It has gained a new interval.

**ILYAN:** “We did not just follow the signal.”

**MARA:** “What did we do?”

**ILYAN:** “We completed a measurement.”

**NIA:** “You mean it was waiting for us?”

**ILYAN:** “I mean the result depends on the route we took. That is not the same thing.”

Tomas finds the signal embedded in a diagnostic log from before the ship entered the corridor. The timestamp is wrong by several hours.

**TOMAS:** “This was in the system before the jump.”

**ORI:** “That statement is inconsistent with the recorded diagnostic sequence.”

**TOMAS:** “Then the sequence is wrong.”

ORI pauses longer than usual.

**ORI:** “I cannot verify that.”

## SEQUENCE 10 — THE SHIP ANSWERS (33:00–37:00)

The forward sensor array activates without a command. Power draw increases. Tomas isolates the circuit manually. The signal is now visible in the sensor's thermal pattern, not as a message but as a repeating heat distribution.

**TOMAS:** “It is not transmitting through the antenna.”

**ILYAN:** “Then where?”

**TOMAS:** “The mount. The metal itself.”

Sera notices the vibration pattern is synchronised with the sample container. She orders the sample sealed and moved away from the array.

**SERA:** “No one touches the material until we know whether it is carrying the pattern.”

Mara orders the array powered down. The heat pattern remains for three seconds after power is cut.

**ORI:** “Signal persists without power input.”

A beat.

**MARA:** “Record everything. No one interprets it as an invitation.”

## SEQUENCE 11 — A HUMAN DECISION (37:00–40:00)

The crew votes on whether to leave immediately or hold position long enough for a passive observation. Mara chooses a short observation window with a strict abort condition. This resolves the immediate conflict: they will not allow curiosity to overrule safety, but they will not discard evidence.

Sera asks that the system be logged as “uncharacterised,” not “uninhabited.” Mara changes the report.

**MARA:** “Put that in the record.”

**SERA:** “You are changing the official language?”

**MARA:** “I am correcting it.”

Ilyan returns to the signal. He notices a coordinate interval forming from the new phase sequence. It points away from the system, but the destination cannot yet be resolved.

## SEQUENCE 12 — THE FIRST COORDINATE (40:00–42:00)

**INT. BRIDGE.**

The crew watches the signal settle into a stable pattern. ORI compares it against the ship's own navigation record.

**ORI:** “A coordinate sequence has resolved.”

**MARA:** “Destination?”

**ORI:** “Not in any known chart.”

**ILYAN:** “It is not pointing to a place.”

**NIA:** “Then what is it pointing to?”

Ilyan looks at the phase trace. One interval changes as Mara steps closer to the console.

**ILYAN:** “A decision.”

The signal resolves into a final pulse. ORI displays a single line in the ship's private system log. Add the text as an editorial graphic, not generated image content:

**“COORDINATE ONE: THE ROUTE YOU REFUSED.”**

Mara stares at it.

**MARA:** “I have never seen this route.”

**ORI:** “The route is associated with a navigation decision in your sealed mission record.”

Mara's expression changes—not fear, but recognition that something has accessed information it should not possess.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**AU009 — MUSIC.** The three-note motif resolves into a fourth note, quiet and unresolved. End without a loud sting.

## Director's continuity notes

- The signal changes only after a measurable event; avoid magical random flicker.
- Mara's sealed mission record is not explained yet.
- ORI is constrained and uncertain; never omniscient.
- The Wayfarer sustains minor sensor damage that persists into Episode 2.
- The sample container's vibration is a clue, not proof of life.
- Register LOC001 and approve the Wayfarer master design before final image generation.
